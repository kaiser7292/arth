Arth can build four in-depth reports from your own data. Each one runs on your phone and can be saved as a PDF.

## Where to find them

**Home tab → Insights page → Reports.**

## The four reports

### Financial health

Needs no input. Covers your net worth, savings rate, debt-to-income, emergency buffer (fixed deposits count towards it) and monthly EMI, with its own grade card across savings rate, emergency fund, debt management, spending discipline and diversification. (This is separate from the plan-based grade on the Goals tab - see [Financial Health grade explained](financial-health-grade).)

### Retirement readiness

Asks a few questions - your age, when you want to retire, monthly expenses today (excluding EMIs), and the returns you expect before and after retirement. It then works out:

- the **corpus** you'd need and how far along you are,
- the **monthly SIP** needed to fill the gap,
- conservative, moderate and optimistic scenarios, a phase-by-phase plan, and the risks to watch.

It uses your [insurance policies](risk-coverage) too - adequate health cover improves your readiness score.

### Loan payoff strategy

Compares paying off your loans **avalanche** style (highest interest first) against **snowball** (smallest balance first), with and without extra payments. You see the interest saved, the payoff timeline and the best order to clear your loans. Needs at least one active loan - see [Loans and EMI tracking](loans).

### Spending personality

Needs no input. Gives your spending an archetype, and shows your top categories and merchants, the monthly trend, which days of the week you spend most, how consistent you are, and behavioural shifts worth noticing.

## Save as PDF

Tap **Download PDF** at the bottom of any report. Arth creates the PDF on your phone and opens the share sheet so you can save or send it. Your answers to the retirement and loan questions are remembered for next time.

> A PDF contains your financial details. Share it only with people you trust.

These reports are estimates based on the numbers you've recorded and the assumptions you enter. They are not financial advice.
