Privacy Policy & Terms of Use

Arth is built on a simple principle: your financial data belongs to you, stored only on your device. These documents explain what that means in practice.

Privacy Policy
Effective: 29 October 2026

This policy covers Arth, the Android app published by Sourav Baid (“we”). Arth has no user accounts and no servers that can read your data. Your financial records are stored in a database on your phone, and we cannot see them. If you join a household (optional), what you choose to share travels end-to-end encrypted through a relay that cannot read it; see Households below.

Data stored on your device

Transactions, accounts, balances, budgets, loans, investments, family ledger (Hisaab) entries, reminders, settings, any passwords you save in the Vault, and what members of your household share with you are stored only on your device. Vault entries and broker credentials are encrypted with a key protected by Android’s secure keystore. None of this is sent to us.

SMS messages

If you allow it, Arth reads the SMS messages on your phone to find bank, card and UPI transaction alerts. It keeps only messages from banks and payment services and ignores personal messages. From those alerts it extracts the amount, date, merchant, account or card last 4 digits and balance, and uses them to add your transactions and keep account balances up to date. Recognised alerts are stored on your device so you can review them. SMS messages are processed only on your device and are never uploaded, shared or sent to any server, including ours. You can turn SMS reading off in Settings, and revoke the permission in Android settings, at any time.

Features that use the internet

Arth only goes online for the features below, and only when you use them. Broker connections use your own API credentials (“bring your own key”), created in your broker’s developer console; your use of them is governed by your broker’s terms, and Arth is not affiliated with any broker. Broker connections are read-only: Arth never places orders or moves money.

  • Zerodha Kite (Kite Connect terms, Zerodha privacy policy). Your Kite API key and API secret are stored encrypted on your device. When you connect, you sign in on Zerodha’s own login page, and Arth exchanges the one-time login code for an access token directly with Zerodha. Your API secret is never sent anywhere; only a one-way checksum made from it goes to Zerodha. Arth then fetches your holdings and positions directly from Zerodha. None of this passes through any server of ours.
  • Angel One (terms, privacy policy) and Zebpay (legal & privacy). The API keys, secrets, client ID, PIN and 2FA (TOTP) secret you enter are sent directly to Angel One or Zebpay to fetch your holdings. They are stored only on your device, encrypted, so Arth can refresh your session, and never pass through our servers.
  • Vault autofill for Kite. If you link a Vault entry to Kite, Arth fills your Zerodha user ID, password and 2FA code into Zerodha’s login page on your device. They go only to Zerodha.
  • Reading payment pictures. When you add a transaction from a picture, the text in it is read on your device by Google’s ML Kit, which runs through Google Play services. The picture and the text read from it never leave your phone. Google Play services may download the text-reading model and send Google diagnostic and usage information about the reading itself (such as your phone’s model and Android version, Arth’s version, how long reading took and any error codes) under Google’s privacy policy; see ML Kit’s data disclosure.
  • New versions (Android). To tell you when a newer Arth is out, Arth asks the Play Store app on your phone (for a Play Store install) or GitHub’s public release list (for an APK installed from GitHub, at most once a day). No information about you or your data is sent; like any website, GitHub sees your phone’s IP address. You can turn this off in Settings.
  • Voice entry. Voice entry uses your phone’s speech recognition service (usually Google). Depending on your phone’s settings, that service may process your voice on its own servers under its own privacy policy. Arth receives only the recognised text.

Each of these services has its own privacy policy, which applies to the data you send them.

Households (optional)

If you start or join a household, you and up to four others can see shared spending together. Nothing is shared just by joining.

  • What is sent. Only what you choose: transactions you mark shared (amount, your share, date, time, merchant, category and description) and how they are split; a monthly savings rate or account balances (name, type and balance), if you turn those on; your Hisaab entries with the members of your household; settle-ups, a household budget and shared goals you create; the name you give yourself; and your sharing choices. Never sent: transactions you keep personal, income, SMS text, account or card numbers, the Vault, loans or broker data.
  • How. Everything is encrypted on your phone (XSalsa20-Poly1305, signed with Ed25519) with a key that only your household’s phones have, before it leaves. Neither we nor our hosting provider can read it. Joining needs an invite from the household and approval on the inviting phone, and both phones show a check code to compare.
  • The relay. Encrypted changes pass through a relay we run on Cloudflare (Cloudflare privacy policy), our hosting provider. It stores only encrypted data, random household and member identifiers, and when and how much was sent. Cloudflare sees your phone’s IP address when it connects, as with any website; the relay does not store it. There are no analytics or logs of what is sent.
  • How long. Each change is deleted from the relay once every phone in the household has it, and in any case after 90 days. Invites expire after 24 hours. A household no phone has used for 12 months is deleted.
  • The other members. What you share is stored on their phones, under their control, like the rest of their Arth data. Removing someone changes the household’s key so they cannot read anything sent afterwards; what they already had stays on their phone.
  • Leaving. Leave in Settings → Household: your shared data is deleted from the relay at once and from the other phones when they next sync. Whoever started the household can close it, which deletes it from the relay. Splits and Hisaab entries already on your phone stay, because they record real money between you.

Other permissions

  • Calendar, only if you turn on calendar sync, to add bills, EMIs and reminders to the calendar you choose.
  • Microphone, only while you use voice entry.
  • Camera, also to scan a household invite QR code, read on your device. Camera and photos, only when you add a transaction from a picture. The picture you choose, share to Arth or take is read on your device to fill in the transaction, and is never uploaded or sent anywhere. Arth sees only pictures you pick; it has no access to the rest of your photos. If you link your UPI IDs to accounts or teach Arth a picture layout, those are stored on your device like the rest of your data.
  • Notifications, alarms and start on boot, for reminders and the daily summary.
  • Fingerprint or face, for app lock. Biometric data is handled by Android and never seen by Arth.

Backups

Backups are encrypted files (AES-GCM) saved on your device or wherever you choose to put them, such as your Downloads folder or a cloud drive you share them to. We never receive them.

Analytics, ads and tracking

Arth contains no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking libraries. We do not sell or share your data, and we have no visibility into how you use the app.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays on your device until you delete it. You can delete individual records in the app (deleted items are kept in the Recycle Bin for 30 days), disconnect any broker in Settings, leave a household, or remove everything by clearing Arth’s storage or uninstalling the app. We hold none of your data in a form we can read; the only thing on our side is a household’s encrypted changes, deleted as described under Households.

Delete your Arth data

Arth (by Sourav Baid) has no user accounts and no servers that can read your data. Everything Arth keeps is on your phone, so you can delete it yourself at any time, without asking us.

In a household? Leave it first (Settings → Household → Leave household). That deletes what you shared from Arth’s relay at once and from the other members’ phones when they next sync. Uninstalling without leaving also works: what you shared is deleted from the relay within 90 days.

Delete everything

  1. Optional: if you want a copy first, open Arth → Settings → Backup & Restore and create a backup.
  2. Open your phone’s Settings → Apps → Arth → Storage and tap Clear storage, or simply uninstall Arth.

This permanently deletes all transactions, accounts, budgets, loans, goals, Hisaab entries, reminders, settings, saved broker keys, the Vault and scan logs. Nothing is kept by us, because we never had it.

When you read a payment picture, Google Play services may send Google diagnostic information about the reading (not the picture or its text; see Reading payment pictures above). Arth never receives it; Google keeps it under its privacy policy, and you can manage what Google keeps about your device in your Google account.

Delete part of your data

  • Individual records: delete them in the app. They move to the Recycle Bin (Settings → Recycle Bin) and are erased for good after 30 days, or immediately if you empty it.
  • Broker connections: open the Zerodha, Angel One or Zebpay credentials screen and tap Remove (Clear for Zerodha) to delete the saved keys and login details. Then delete or regenerate the API key in that broker’s developer console.
  • Backups: backup files are saved wherever you put them; delete them there.

What is kept elsewhere

Data you sent to a broker (Zerodha, Angel One or Zebpay) when connecting is held by that broker under its own privacy policy; contact the broker to delete it. Reports you chose to email us are kept in our inbox until you ask us to delete them.
