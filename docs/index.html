Sound familiar?

Your money is spread across your life. Arth puts it back together.

“Where did it all go?”
Six banking apps, forty transaction messages a week, and a card bill that still surprises you. Arth turns the messages you already get into one picture, without your bank password.
“Who paid for what?”
Rent on your card, groceries on theirs, a trip paid from both, and a chat thread of “I'll send it later”. Arth splits a bill once and keeps both phones agreeing on who owes whom.
“Are we on track?”
The car, the trip, the home loan, retirement: guessed at, never planned. Arth plans your year, projects the month, and shows whether your goals will actually land.
Every day

One message. Everything updates.

Scroll to follow a single transaction SMS all the way through.

STEP 01

A transaction SMS arrives.

The same SMS you already ignore a dozen times a week. You don't open the app, and you don't type anything.

STEP 02

Arth works out what it was.

It reads the amount and the account, recognises the merchant, and categorises the spend, then puts the result in a queue, not in your books. A format it hasn't seen? Show it one message and it reads that format from then on.

STEP 03

You approve it. It lands.

One tap and the expense is recorded against the right card, on the right date, in the right category. You can edit it, or reject it outright if it isn't a valid spend.

STEP 04

Everything downstream moves with it.

The category budget, the month's pace, the projected total and your closing balance all recalculate. That's the whole loop — and you typed nothing.

Every day, without you

Teach it once. It never asks again.

Each of these takes about a minute to configure. After that they run on their own.

STEP 01

Write one rule. It runs forever.

When a transaction matches, set the category, approve it without asking, and split it with whoever it should be split with. Rules stack in priority order.

STEP 02

For the spends no SMS will catch.

Cash, the autorickshaw, a friend covering lunch. Say it out loud and it is logged with the amount, merchant, category and account already filled in.

STEP 03

Shared money keeps its own books.

Anything split routes into that person's ledger on its own, carrying a running balance you can settle or export whenever you want to.

Every month

Then it starts telling you things.

Capture is the boring half. This is the half that changes what you do.

STEP 01

It notices what you don't.

Sunday is your most expensive day of the week, by a factor of two. Nobody works that out by staring at a bank statement.

STEP 02

It tells you where the month lands.

Planned against actual, line by line, with the variance on each one — while there are still days left to do something about it.

STEP 03

It lets you test a decision first.

Model a raise, a new EMI or an early prepayment, and watch the next six months redraw before you commit to any of it.

STEP 04

And the things you'd rather not hear.

No term cover. Savings rate under 30%. Corpus runs dry in 13 years. Blunt, specific, and with the arithmetic shown.

Together

Your money stays yours. What you share, you share together.

Rent on one card, groceries on the other, the holiday from both. Arth keeps one honest picture for the people you live with: no joint account, no shared password, no spreadsheet.

7:42 PM

Jane buys groceries on her card.

The transaction SMS arrives and Arth has it ready in Catch Up: amount, shop, category.

7:42 PM

She splits it with John, half each.

One tap. Her budget counts her half; John owes her the rest.

7:43 PM

It travels locked.

Sealed on Jane's phone, opened only on John's. Arth's relay in between can't read it.

7:43 PM

John's phone files his half.

In his budget under Groceries, in his Hisaab with Jane. Nobody typed anything twice.

SUNDAY

He settles up. Both phones agree.

All square on both sides, and the Goa trip they're saving for keeps moving.

Private by default. Shared by choice.
